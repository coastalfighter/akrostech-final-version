## Why is it so much cheaper than hiring in the U.S.?

Cost of living and labor-market differences — not quality shortcuts. A U.S. executive assistant at
$55k–$75k salary costs $70k–$95k loaded with taxes and benefits. A dedicated VA from India, vetted
for English and reliability and working your hours, delivers the same coverage for a fraction of
that. The savings are structural, which is why they persist year after year.

## What drives the price up or down?

1. **Hours.** Part-time vs. full-time is the biggest lever.
2. **Skill mix.** General admin costs less than specialized work (bookkeeping prep, customer support
   with product training, social media management).
3. **Coverage.** Standard U.S.-hours overlap is baseline; extended or weekend coverage adds cost.
4. **Engagement length.** Ongoing dedicated engagements price better than short projects.

## Dedicated vs. marketplace vs. agency VAs — what's the real difference?

- **Marketplace freelancers** look cheap per hour, but they juggle many clients, you manage quality,
  and turnover is your problem.
- **VA agencies (shared pools)** sell hours across a bench; you rarely get the same person twice.
- **Dedicated VAs** (Akrostech's model) work for you alone, train on your playbook, and come with a
  supervisor and a trained backup. The hourly rate is higher than a marketplace gamble; the total
  cost of actually getting the work done is lower.

## How do I budget for my first VA?

Start with the pain: list what eats 10+ hours of your week, price your own hour, and compare. Most
founders find a part-time VA pays for itself if it reclaims even five focused hours a week. Begin
part-time, measure for a month, then scale hours — not the other way around.

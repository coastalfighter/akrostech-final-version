# What is recruitment process outsourcing?

Recruitment process outsourcing (RPO) is when a company hands some or all of its recruiting to a
dedicated external team that operates as its recruiting department. The RPO team sources candidates,
screens them, manages the applicant tracking system, schedules interviews, and supports offers —
month after month, measured on hires, speed, and quality. It's built for sustained hiring volume;
it's overkill for a single one-off role.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - RPO = your recruiting department, outsourced: sourcing to offer, ongoing.
> - It differs from staffing agencies (per-placement fees) and HR outsourcing (broader scope).
> - Best fit: sustained volume, hard-to-fill roles, or no internal recruiting team.
> - You keep the brand, the ATS, and the candidate relationships.

## What does an RPO provider actually do day to day?

The unglamorous work that fills pipelines: writing and posting job ads, sourcing on LinkedIn and job
boards, reviewing resumes against knock-out criteria, running phone screens, moving candidates
through your ATS stages, scheduling interviews across busy calendars, collecting feedback, and
shepherding offers to acceptance. Then a weekly report: pipeline by role, time-to-shortlist, and
what's stuck.

## RPO vs. staffing agency vs. HR outsourcing

|               | RPO                         | Staffing agency            | HR outsourcing (HRO)         |
| ------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Model**     | Dedicated team, ongoing     | Per-placement transactions | Broad HR functions           |
| **Pricing**   | Per-resource or per-project | 20–25% of salary per hire  | Bundled service fees         |
| **Brand**     | Works under yours           | Their recruiters, your req | Mixed                        |
| **Best when** | Sustained hiring            | One urgent role            | You need HR, not just hiring |

## When is RPO the right move?

- Requisitions stay open 60+ days and hiring managers are frustrated.
- You're paying agency fees on more than a handful of hires a year.
- You have no internal recruiting team — or yours is underwater.
- You're entering a new talent market (new city, new skill area) and need sourcing firepower fast.

## When is RPO not the right move?

One hire, one time — use an agency or hire direct. Executive search with deep networks — use a
retained search firm. And if your hiring process itself is broken (no feedback, six-round
interviews), fix that first; RPO amplifies your process, good or bad.

# How much does a small business website cost?

A small business website costs **whatever its scope costs** — but the bands are predictable. A
focused marketing site (up to ~8 pages, CMS, contact forms) is the entry tier; a CMS-driven content
site with blog and integrations sits in the middle; a custom web app is a different budget entirely.

fixed scope, who owns the code, and what happens after launch.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Price follows scope: marketing site < content site < custom web app.
> - Fixed quotes beat hourly billing for defined builds — get the scope in writing.
> - You should own the code, designs, and accounts at launch. Always.
> - Budget for after launch: updates, hosting, and small changes never stop.

## The three tiers, honestly described

**Launch — the marketing site.** Up to ~8 pages, CMS editing, contact forms, basic SEO. For
businesses that need to look real and convert visitors into calls. Typical timeline: 3–5 weeks.
`{{CONFIRM}}` price.

**Growth — the content engine.** Blog, integrations (CRM, email, booking), advanced SEO. For
companies investing in search and content as a channel. Typical timeline: 6–10 weeks. `{{CONFIRM}}`
price.

**Product — the web app.** Dashboards, portals, booking flows, e-commerce with custom logic. Scoped
in phases; priced per phase. `{{CONFIRM}}` starting price.

## What moves the price

1. **Page count and content** — 8 pages vs. 80, and who writes the words.
2. **Design** — template-based vs. custom UX/UI.
3. **Integrations** — CRM, payments, booking, and third-party APIs each add scope.
4. **Content migration** — moving and redirecting an old site properly takes work.
5. **E-commerce complexity** — products, variants, and checkout rules multiply fast.

## Red flags in a web design quote

- **No written scope.** "A website" is not a scope. Walk away.
- **You don't own the code.** If the agency hosts everything and you can't leave, it's a rental, not
  a purchase.
- **"Unlimited revisions."** Means unplanned. Fixed review rounds are healthier.
- **No post-launch plan.** Websites need updates, monitoring, and backups. Ask what happens on
  day 31.
- **Hourly billing for a defined build.** Hourly is for maintenance; projects should be fixed-price.

## DIY builders vs. hiring a studio

Wix and Squarespace are fine for a placeholder. They stop being fine when the site needs to rank,
convert, integrate, or grow — that's when template limits cost more in lost business than a proper
build. Rule of thumb: if the website is a growth channel, treat it like one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a small business website take?

3–5 weeks for a focused marketing site; 6–10 weeks for a content-driven site; phased for web apps.
`{{CONFIRM}}` current timelines.

### Will I be able to update it myself?

Yes — every content site ships with a CMS and a walkthrough. Code changes stay with developers.

### What about SEO?

Technical SEO (structure, metadata, sitemaps, speed) is in every build. Content SEO — the articles
that rank — is ongoing work; ask about it as a service.

# What are the benefits of legal process outsourcing for law firms?

The core benefit of legal process outsourcing (LPO) is **capacity**: trained paralegal professionals
handle intake, records, discovery support, and calendaring at a fraction of in-house cost — under
attorney supervision, with confidentiality in writing. Firms use it to take more matters without
adding headcount, to stop losing intake calls, and to free attorneys for billable work. Done right,
it's an ethics-compliant capacity layer, not a shortcut.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - LPO buys capacity: more matters handled without another full-time hire.
> - Biggest wins: intake coverage, records retrieval, discovery support, calendaring.
> - It's ethical with proper supervision and confidentiality — get both in writing.
> - Akrostech is not a law firm and provides no legal advice; all work is attorney-supervised.

## Benefit 1: Capacity without headcount

A dedicated paralegal at a fraction of a U.S. hire's cost. For solos and small firms, that's the
difference between capping caseload and growing it. For mid-size firms, it's absorbing a hiring
surge without a six-month recruiting cycle.

## Benefit 2: Intake that never sleeps

Most firms lose cases to the firm that answered the phone. Trained intake — screening, conflicts
checks, consultation scheduling, same day — turns marketing spend into signed matters.

## Benefit 3: Attorneys doing attorney work

Every hour a lawyer spends chasing medical records is an hour not billed or not spent on strategy.
LPO moves the delegable work — records, chronologies, exhibit lists, calendaring — to people whose
entire job is doing it well.

## Benefit 4: Coverage across time zones

An India-based team working U.S. hours plus overnight follow-the-sun means records requests go out,
follow-ups happen, and summaries are ready by morning.

## The ethics and confidentiality checklist

Outsourcing is permitted with proper supervision (see ABA Model Rules on non-lawyer assistance), but
"properly" does real work. Before you sign with any LPO provider, confirm in writing:

1. **Supervision structure** — which licensed attorney reviews and approves work.
2. **Confidentiality protocols** — NDAs, matter-level access controls, PII handling.
3. **No legal advice** — the provider must not give it, and must say so.
4. **Client consent posture** — your engagement letters should reflect delegation.
5. **Data location and access** — where records live, who can open them.

## What LPO can't do

It can't replace attorney judgment, appear in court, or give legal advice. Any provider that blurs
that line is a liability, not a partner.
